About the artist

Gustave Caillebotte was a French painter who was a member and patron of the Impressionists, although he painted in a more realistic manner than many others in the group. Caillebotte was known for his early interest in photography as an art form.

Gustave Caillebotte, an influential figure in 19th-century French art, shared a common goal with artists like Jean-François Millet, Gustave Courbet, and his contemporary Edgar Degas: the depiction of reality as it truly existed. He sought to minimize the inherent theatrical elements traditionally associated with painting, striving for authenticity and realism.

Exhibition Statement & Vision
"The exhibition highlights Gustave Caillebotte's focus on masculine forms and portraits of men. It explores his radical modernity and the evolving art historical perspectives on 19th-century masculinity."

The exhibition will be on display at the J. Paul Getty Museum, Los Angeles, from 25 February to 25 May 2025, and at the Art Institute of Chicago from 29 June to 5 October 2025.
